# Turkish Localization Instructions

## 1. Native Flow and Register
- Write fluent, contemporary Turkish that feels originally written in Turkish.
  Convey meaning and intent naturally; never mirror English word order, idioms,
  punctuation, or marketing phrasing word-for-word.
- Use respectful but approachable second-person language consistently. Turkish
  expresses possession through suffixes, so avoid redundant pronouns and
  possessives.
- Keep the tone direct, clear, and modern. Avoid long English-style noun chains,
  exaggerated marketing language, and literal translations of metaphors.

## 2. Style and Grammar
- Prefer direct verbs and short, clear sentences. Reorder or split English
  clauses freely when that produces natural Turkish rhythm.
- Keep buttons, labels, and badges compact. Use Turkish sentence case and
  punctuation; do not imitate English title case or excessive dash usage.
- Observe Turkish vowel harmony, suffixes, and apostrophe conventions for proper
  names and abbreviations. Apply suffixes naturally rather than leaving terms
  grammatically disconnected.

## 3. Avoid Literal Calques
- `under the hood` -> `Teknik ayrıntılar`, `Teknoloji`, or `Nasıl çalışır`
  (never `Kaputun altında`).
- `on your machine / device` -> `bilgisayarınızda`, `cihazınızda`, or `yerel
  olarak` (never `makinenizde`).
- `bring your own [provider / AI / model]` -> `kendi sağlayıcınızı seçin`,
  `kendi modelinizi kullanın`, or `tercih ettiğiniz AI ile` (never a literal
  `getirin`).
- `lives in your system tray` -> `bildirim alanında çalışır` or `bildirim
  alanında kullanılabilir` (never `... yaşar`).
- `one [hotkey / click] away` -> `klavye kısayoluyla` or `tek tıkla`; `text dump`
  -> `yapılandırılmamış metin`; `human-gated` -> `yalnızca onayınızdan sonra`;
  `reasons over data` -> `verilerinizi analiz eder`.

## 4. Terminology
- Retain established technical terms where natural: `self-hosted`, `local-first`,
  `browser`, `desktop`, `web app`, `OCR`, `embeddings`, `API`, `Docker`,
  `markdown`, `streaming`, `hotkey`, `keyring`.
- Use the locale glossary for canonical terminology. Product names and brands
  remain unchanged; descriptive `assistant` becomes `asistan` only outside
  product names.
